What does "being apolitical" mean?

Research Problem

People identify themselves as apolitical, however each person may interpret this positioning differently.

Are they apolitical or is their political participation different from the generally perceived model?

Yelnaz Kuralkanova | Fall 2026 | PLS 210

Research Question

"What does 'being apolitical' mean to people who do not actively participate in politics in Kazakhstan?"

Focus: how inactive political participants interpret their identity, participation, and relationship with politics in the Kazakhstani context.

Epistemological Positioning: Interpretivism

  • Focuses on what something means to people experiencing it.
  • Explores how people interpret the identity of being "apolitical".
  • Does not measure participation or follow cause-and-effect logic.

Literature Review: Non-participation = Apolitical

Tolen et al. (2023)
Low involvement in formal political institutions can represent political disengagement and political absenteeism among youth.
Kilybayeva et al. (2017)
Limited participation and weak involvement in political institutions may reflect distance and detachment from politics.
Dosanova (2020)
Weak institutional trust, low motivation, and feelings that politics is ineffective can lead to political disengagement.

Literature Review: Non-participation ≠ Apolitical

Zholdybalina, Yermakhanova & Akhantayeva (2026)
Participation can happen through social initiatives, community activities, and digital engagement.
Aitymbetov, Tolen & Ismagambetov (2023)
Digital spaces create new forms of political involvement through opinions, discussions, and online expression.
Zholdybalina & Yermakhanova
Informal activities can represent alternative forms of civic participation beyond elections and parties.

Debate & Research Gap

  • What are causes of non-participation?
  • Does it necessarily mean not caring about politics?
  • Is it possible to not care about politics?

Research gap: Existing research measured participation, but rarely asked inactive participants themselves how they define "being apolitical".

Analytical Framework: Sensitising Concepts

  • Political disengagement
  • Political trust
  • Belonging and responsibility
  • Everyday forms of participation
  • Political background

Central idea: Identity construction — what does it mean to be apolitical?

Expectations, and I could be wrong about them

  • Non-participants may not be apolitical but may hide behind the label.
  • They may not want to identify as political activists.
  • They may believe they cannot influence politics.
  • People may interpret "apolitical" differently.

Benefit: understanding political terms and context.
